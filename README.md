# Customer-Banking-Insights-
Built an interactive Power BI dashboard analyzing 4,000+ customer records across UK: Visualized gender distribution, customer balance trends, job classification insight, implemented filters &amp; slicers (region, age group, job classification, month) for dynamic analysis, delivered actionable insights such as demographic-based trends.

📘 Overview

This Power BI dashboard provides an in-depth analysis of customer banking insights across the United Kingdom. It visualizes key customer demographics, financial balances, and growth trends to help understand customer behavior, regional distribution, and financial patterns.

🎯 Objectives

To analyze customer balances across regions, genders, and job classifications.

To understand the contribution of different age groups to total bank balances.

To identify growth trends in customer acquisition over quarters.

To provide actionable insights for improving customer engagement and retention strategies.

🧩 Dashboard Features
Page 1 – Overview
Key Metrics

Total Customers: 4,014

Total Balance: £159.62M

Visual Insights

Gender Distribution by Region

England shows an almost equal male-female split (≈50%).

Scotland and Wales have higher male percentages, while Northern Ireland has more female customers.

Customer Balance Distribution (Pie Chart)

Males hold 54.28% of the total balance (£86.64M).

Females hold 45.72% (£72.98M).

Balance by Job Classification

White Collar: £78M (highest).

Blue Collar: £41M.

Other: £40M.

Additional Insights

Customer balances range from £38.5K to £40.5K across age groups.

Total male customers: 2,165; Total female customers: 1,849.

Page 2 – Age and Growth Insights

Sum of Balance by Age Group

26–40 years contribute the highest balance (£86M, over 50% of total).

15–25 years have the lowest (£12M).

Customer Growth by Quarters

Strongest growth observed in Q4 with 1,613 new sign-ups.

Steady rise from 123 in Q1 to 1,613 in Q4.

Average of Balance by Age Group

15–25 years: £38.5K (lowest).

26–40 years: £39.9K.

41–52 years: £39.5K.

Others: £40.6K (highest).

💡 Key Insights Summary

The 26–40 age group is the bank’s most valuable segment.

White-collar professionals hold nearly half of all balances.

England and Scotland have strong male customer representation.

Quarter 4 marks the highest customer acquisition period.
